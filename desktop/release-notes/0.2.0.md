First public release of Rig Doctor Globe. It shows where your game traffic goes on a 3D globe, measures League of Legends NA in real matches on the direct route and through a relay, and its Auto mode picks the relay only when your matches show it is 10 ms faster.

## Relay

- Turn a WireGuard relay on or off from the app with one control (#24, #79). Only traffic to Riot's network goes through it (#17).
- Join a relay with a one-time invite. Keys are made on your device and only the public key is sent (#68).
- Auto decides from League's own in-match ping. It compares your last 10 matches on each route and takes the relay only when it wins by 10 ms or more, with at least 3 matches on each (#64, #65).
- Auto never switches from ready check to the end of a match. Your own On, Off and Connect still work mid-match, and On and Connect warn you first (#57, #62).
- A match where the relay changed mid-match is marked mixed and left out of Auto and the monthly verdict (#80).
- On macOS the League tunnel narrows to the game-server ranges seen in your matches. Windows keeps the relay's full Riot list so new ranges never ask for admin approval again (#71, #81).

## Proof

- League's in-game ping is logged for each match with the route it used, read from League's own log after the match. The Relay card shows the last one (#59).
- This month's verdict under the relay controls: no 10 ms gain, or fewer than 3 matches on either route, means a refund is due. Nothing is billed yet (#31, #40, #59, #65).
- The Proof panel compares the direct and relay routes to the same server and keeps every comparison (#22, #27, #45).

## Globe

- Globe-first layout with side panels that float and collapse (#72).
- 3D route arcs and server boxes, with labels checked for overlap in CI (#14, #32).
- Live connections and packet flow. Hover a process to highlight its routes (#74, #75, #76).
- A scheduled peak-hour trace log (#15).
- Riot's public NA sites are labeled Reference: they come from public information, not from a measurement on your PC (#82).

## macOS

- Live connections, traces, pings and the League game server on macOS, in an Apple Silicon .dmg (#19, #20, #23).

## Privacy and safety

- GeoIP runs offline from DB-IP Lite, so the addresses on your route never go to a lookup service. The app asks api.ipify.org, or Cloudflare as a fallback, for your own public address (#21, #49).
- On Windows the tunnel service reads an admin-protected copy of the config (#48).
- The app and the macOS helper refuse a relay config whose ranges add up to more than about a million IPv4 addresses, so no config can route a large part of the internet through the relay (#82).
- Trace and ping targets are validated, dependencies are license and advisory checked, and third-party notices ship in the installers (#50, #51).

## Known limits

- The Windows installer isn't code-signed yet. If SmartScreen stops it, choose More info, then Run anyway.
- The relay needs WireGuard: the official installer on Windows, `brew install wireguard-tools` on macOS.
- Relay access is by invite while a small group tests it.
- Whether the relay helps depends on how your provider reaches Riot. On the developer's own line both routes measured about 30 ms in matches: a tie.
- Match proof hasn't been tested on a real Mac yet.
